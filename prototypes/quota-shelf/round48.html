Round 48 · what the shelf says when it does not know

Every round so far draws a shelf that is certain. The payload is present, fresh and complete, and the bars are drawn with total confidence. Three things break that and none has ever been drawn.

Why stale is the dangerous one

We already know payloads rot within about 28 hours: the resets drift into the past, print now, and the paces clamp to zero. A clamped pace is not a neutral value. Pace is the share of the window still to run, so a pace of zero means "the window is over", and fill > pace is then true for every lane with any credit left at all. The shelf lights up amber across the board and looks like a crisis. It is the most dangerous state on this list because it is the one that looks most like information. Below, the same live payload with every reset moved one minute into the past.

The fix is a bug fix, not a style. clamp() floors at zero, so a passed reset does not produce no pace — it produces a pace of exactly zero, and zero is a meaningful value on that axis. A reset in the past means the window has turned over and we know nothing about the current one. That is the absence of a pace, and the shelf has drawn that since round 33: Antigravity states no window length, gets pace: null, and renders a flat bar with no cut, no hatch and no doom. Routing the stale case into that existing path removes the false crisis at the source instead of dimming it.

How doubt is drawn

Amber and red are the only saturated marks on this shelf and they always mean a problem with the quota. Not knowing is not a quota problem, so it may not borrow those colours — a stale shelf that goes amber is lying in exactly the same voice it uses to tell the truth. So doubt removes confidence rather than adding alarm: the lanes fade toward the rail they sit on, and one line of plain grey says the age.

Three depths, because both ends fail. Too subtle and a day-old shelf passes for a live one. Too strong and a shelf nine minutes behind looks broken.
