Round 36 · a lane for a balance with no maximum

Every other lane on the shelf is a proportion. A window grants an allowance, the allowance runs down, and the lane draws what is left of it. OpenRouter grants nothing. You top it up, you spend it, and the only two facts are a number of dollars and the direction of travel.

A bar is a ratio, and a ratio needs a denominator. Today's row draws $0.90 as a short bar, which silently invents a maximum the account does not have. That is not wrong by a little — the same balance would draw at a different length on a different day for no reason anyone could name.

It also breaks the risk vocabulary, and that part is good news. Credit bought outright never expires, so an OpenRouter lane can never carry the amber loss mark. Exactly one thing can go wrong here: it runs out. One failure, one colour.

What each denominator costs

Four of the five need the proxy to remember something. An unbounded balance cannot be drawn as a proportion from a single reading. Either the router keeps history, or this row can only ever show an absolute number — the one thing the shelf has banned.
