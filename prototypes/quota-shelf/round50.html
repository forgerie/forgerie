Round 50 · the whole shelf, consolidated

Thirty-five rounds of decisions in one page. This is not a new proposal — it is what shipped, drawn from the live payload, so the prototype and the plugin can be held against each other. Every constant here has the same value it has in quota-shelf.js.

The shelf answers three questions and refuses every other job: what can I spend now, what expires unspent, what is draining too fast.

The vocabulary

The shelf

Width buys columns

The lanes never move and never re-plot, so a mark learned in a narrow panel means the same thing in a wide one. Only the lane thickness changes, and only because it is free: once any text column shows, the line is already a 12 px box set by the 10 px type.

Height buys names

The address is the only thing costing a whole line per account, and it answers which account rather than whether to act. Under 440 px of room it comes off. Folded landscape is the posture that used to push Codex and OpenRouter off the bottom of the screen.

When the shelf does not know

Amber and red always mean a problem with the quota. Not knowing is not one, so doubt fades rather than reddens — a stale shelf that goes amber lies in the voice it uses to tell the truth.

Structure

chosenbecauseruled out
nest, one lane per account A pooled bar's length is a mean. Two accounts with equal and opposite overshoot average to a lane drawn exactly on pace while one is about to lose nearly everything. A single loss is divided by the account count; two queued losses collapse into one. Nesting never averages, and costs the same up to three accounts — 27 px either way. pool · signed averaging · pooled bar with an unpooled readout · telling the bar how many accounts it pools · a precedence rule between the two risks
an account is always a block, held apart by padding Separating only accounts that own more than one lane made Codex's three one-meter accounts draw at the same 4 px pitch as Claude's three meters — the same picture for two different facts. The padding also gives the block a height, so the routed riser is never a 3 px stub. conditional grouping (r34) · a margin instead of padding
the address above its lanes A column held 132 px away from every lane on the shelf to carry a label that changes once per account, and truncated it anyway. Above, the lanes get the width back and the address is never cut. a fixed left column · trail, inline, big placements · lift, indent, step, split treatments
a boolean is a token, under the account that owns it A pool that answers or refuses reports no level, so a bar would claim a measurement the provider does not make. Collected into a shared strip, the tokens belonged to nobody and had to be read by position. bar, ghost, count, a hairline, a dot-with-wire · a detached roster strip
a balance is a number OpenRouter's share is spend over everything ever topped up, so it rises when you add money and says nothing about capacity. $20 added, $9.11 spent, 45.5% used — the denominator only ever grows. No scale, so no bar. flat, topup, runway, week, dot denominators · line, rail, orb, lamp marks

Measurement

chosenbecauseruled out
doom by rate, fill ÷ pace How many times the window's natural rate you must sustain to waste nothing. Unit-free, so it reads the same on a five-hour lane and a seven-day one. Sooner is not worse: a sliver going in 6 h is less urgent than a fortune going in 48 h. a fixed horizon — it glowed on every five-hour lane for its whole life
solid at every distance, hue amber→red, capped at 8× on a log curve The discriminator for a ramp is its floor, not its ceiling: a ramp that fades to nothing can erase a real warning. Intensity lives in hue, never in fill. fade, pip, weight, hollowing over time
no pace from a passed reset Clamping to zero says "the window is over", which makes fill > pace true for every lane holding credit and paints a merely-stale payload as a shelf-wide crisis. Absence of a pace is what the shelf has drawn since r33 for providers that state no window length. Math.max(0, …) — shipped for months, fixed in f197e49
no runway tick runway < timeLeft reduces algebraically to fill < pace, which is the hatch condition. Swept 400,599 combinations: one disagreement, a rounding tie. No history reaches the shelf, so any rate computed from level, window and reset is a restatement of them. the tick · the runway as a mark (it survives only as a number, in the countdown)

Colour and room

chosenbecauseruled out
amber and red only ever mean a problem Health carries no colour, and neither does doubt. A stale shelf that goes amber lies in exactly the voice it uses to tell the truth. severity colouring on healthy lanes · reddening a stale payload
an arrow on the countdown Amber against red is exactly the pair red-green colour blindness collapses, and the two states are opposite instructions — spend faster, spend slower. Under simulation, 2h and 3d became the same yellow-green. Up is credit dying; down is a lane draining. hue alone · font weight (says which is worse, not which is which)
hue keyed to the meter Keyed by position, a colour would mean "the second thing here" rather than "the week": disable one bucket and every lane below it changes colour. hue by row position
width buys columns, height buys names One state, no toggle. Clock at 430, level at 510, address at 640, meter name at 820; addresses off under 440 px of room. Height is measured from the parent — an unconstrained shelf is exactly as tall as its content, so measuring itself would drop the addresses, get shorter, and agree with itself forever. an expand toggle · slots · columns · segments · viewport breakpoints
one unit, always — 45m, 38h, 3d Two units is four characters of noise when the fact is "about two days". Money follows the same rule: cents below $10, whole dollars above. 1d 14h · $47.31 above ten · 1680m
