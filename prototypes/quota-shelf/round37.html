Round 37 · OpenRouter is not a meter

Round 36 tried five denominators for a balance that has none, and the best of them still drew a position on a scale. A scale is a claim, and from one reading of a prepaid balance there is no claim to make. Ninety cents is not far along anything — it is a number with no axis.

So stop drawing a quantity. From a single reading the only honest facts are that there is money or there is not, which is a boolean, and round 35 already settled what a boolean looks like. OpenRouter is a roster entry, drawn exactly like Antigravity's Gemini: a dash at rest, a red mark when it cannot serve.

Two kinds of lane, not four special cases

Worth noticing: Antigravity's Claude and GPT-OSS lanes are unmeasured for a third reason — they report a percentage but no window length — so two thirds of that row is already pretending to be measured. A percentage with no window is as unplottable as a balance with no maximum.

What this costs

A boolean cannot warn you early. You find out at zero, not before. three is the only treatment that tries, and it buys the warning by inventing a $2 threshold — a far smaller invention than a whole scale, because a threshold only has to be roughly right and a scale has to be right at every point on it.

The real fix is elsewhere: if the router keeps a short spend history, the balance stops being unmeasured and becomes a runway, and this lane earns a cut like any other. That is the same ask as Antigravity's missing window length — one change to the proxy repairs both rows.
