Round 52 · height without collapse

Round 51 proved no single line per provider answers all three questions, because a collapse has to pick one. But the goal was never one line per provider — the goal was height, and height is being spent in places that carry no information.

Only 40 % of the shelf is marks. And of those 13 lanes, ten are healthy right now — a bar at some level with no cut crossed, no hatch, no doom. Each costs exactly what a lane in trouble costs. A shelf whose job is "is anything wrong" spends three-quarters of its height saying no.

Three ways out, none of which averages

dense keeps every lane and strips the chrome: rows are not cards, the tag column is the divider. triage draws by exception — a lane is drawn if it is in trouble or if it is the routed account's binding lane, and the accounts not drawn are counted. tiled puts providers across instead of down, so height is the tallest provider rather than the sum.

The same three on a quiet day

Every lane healthy. dense and tiled cost what they cost. triage costs almost nothing, which is the point: height proportional to trouble, not to roster.

"What can I spend" is answered only for the routed account; the reserves' levels are not shown, only their number. This is a glance, not an audit, and it must say so.
tiled—— Every account, every meter, across. Needs about 220 px per provider. At four providers that is 900 px, which only the unfolded landscape posture has. Below that it has to fall back to one of the others.

The finding

All three answer all three questions, because none of them averages. They differ in what they charge. dense charges the labels. triage charges the healthy accounts' levels. tiled charges width.

They also compose. triage on a dense layout is the floor: a quiet shelf at about 60 px, growing only where something is wrong. And the room rule already shipped decides when to use it — the same measurement that takes the addresses off under 440 px can take the healthy lanes off under 200.
