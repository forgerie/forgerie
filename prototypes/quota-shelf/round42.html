Round 42 · expanded, without re-plotting

The obvious way to build an expanded shelf is to draw a second, bigger thing: cards, headers, a lane per row with its name above it. That spends the one property the collapsed shelf took twenty-five rounds to earn — you would learn the drawing twice, and every mark you trained yourself to read at 360 px would sit somewhere else at 900.

So expansion here adds columns and never moves a lane. Same geometry, same hues, same origin, same doom bar, same hatch. Width buys words; it does not buy a new picture.

This round first claimed expansion was free of height. The render disproved it. A 10 px number needs a 12 px line box and the collapsed lane pitch is 7 px, so the moment any word appears the shelf grows: 144 px → 253 px. What is true, and what the measurement below shows, is that the growth happens once. Every column after the first is free, because they all share that one line box. The first build did not share it, and grew a second time — 228 px, then 260 px — when the taller account column arrived.

The order things arrive in is the order they become worth saying. when first — hours are not a share of anything, so a fraction axis can never hold them. Then level, for when "about half" is not enough. Then account, because nothing in the collapsed drawing identifies one and you need the address to go and switch it. meter last, because hue already separates the three meters inside an account, and nothing at all separates the accounts.

What the countdown is saying

It names whichever fact lands first, and the colour says which kind it is — the same rule the lane itself draws by.

  • fill < pace — less credit left than clock left, so the lane runs dry early. The lane hatches; the countdown is the runway and it is red, because work stops.
  • fill > pace — more credit left than clock left, so credit dies unspent. The lane shows the solid doom bar; the countdown is the reset and it is amber, because money is lost.
  • otherwise — the plain reset, in grey.
  • no stated window — —, because a dash says "no such thing" and an empty cell reads as broken.
