Round 51 · one line per provider

The nested shelf gives every account its own lane and every meter its own line — 13 lines for four providers today, growing with the roster. This asks what a provider looks like on exactly one line, as a denser alternate mode rather than a replacement.

The collapse crosses two dimensions at once, and they are not the same problem. Codex is 3 accounts with 1 meter each, so accounts collapse. Claude is 1 account with 3 meters, so meters collapse. Antigravity is both.

Round 28 ruled out pooling, and the reason is the trap this round walks back into: a pooled bar's length is a mean, and a mean of two opposite errors is no error at all. So every candidate is put through the case that killed it.

Today's shape, five ways

Three providers, 13 lanes in the nested shelf, one line each here.

The cancellation case

One account at 10 % with 90 % of its window still to run — starving, dry long before it resets. One account at 90 % with 10 % left — idle, about to lose almost everything. Pool them and fill lands on 50, pace lands on 50, and the lane draws exactly on pace while one account runs dry and the other wastes a fortune.

The queue case

Two accounts both wasting, at different distances. A mean shows one loss of average size; the truth is two losses.

The finding

No single line answers both questions. "What can I spend now" is answered by the account being spent (pin). "What is about to go wrong" is answered by whichever account is in trouble (worst), and that is usually a different account. A mode that picks one is not a summary of the provider, it is a summary of one question about the provider.

If a dense mode is wanted, worst is the one to build on, because a shelf you glance at is a shelf you are asking "is anything wrong". But it must be readable as that — the line is not the provider's level, and drawing it in the same lane geometry as the nested shelf would say it is.
