Summon any panel with a long press on the conversation. Holding a finger anywhere on the transcript opens a radial menu centred on that point: eight wedges at 45 degrees, holding Inbox, Files, Git, Terminal, two more panels, Notifications, and an overflow to everything else. Slide toward a wedge and release to open it; release in the middle to cancel.

A wedge's direction never changes. Every other arrangement has to rotate to stay on screen near an edge, which means it can only promise an order rather than an angle — a full circle has no direction it can rotate to, so Inbox is west on every press, in every posture, and the gesture becomes learnable by hand. The slot map is stored rather than derived from the installed panel list, so installing or removing a plugin never moves a direction you have already learned, and a panel that is temporarily hidden keeps its slot as a dark, reserved wedge instead of letting the wheel close up around it.

The gesture is touch-only and arms after the same half-second hold the inbox rows already use, so it takes nothing away from dragging a panel in or pressing an opener. The hold is drawn as a closing ring while it counts down. Panels remain reachable by every existing route.
